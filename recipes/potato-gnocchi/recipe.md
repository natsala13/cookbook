# Potato Gnocchi

## Ingredients

- 1,000 g whole, raw, mature starchy potatoes, about 5 potatoes at 200 g each
- 150 g Italian 00 flour or low-protein plain flour
- 10 g additional flour, reserved for correction if the test gnocco fails
- 36 g egg yolk, about 2 large yolks, with no egg white
- 10 g fine sea salt
- 25 g rice flour or semolina, for dusting

## Steps

1. Heat a conventional oven to 210°C, or a fan oven to 200°C. Scrub and dry 1,000 g potatoes; leave them whole, unpeeled, and unwrapped. Pierce each potato twice with a thin skewer.
2. Set the potatoes directly on the oven rack and bake for 55–65 minutes for 200 g potatoes. They are ready when a skewer passes through without resistance and the centers reach 96–98°C; larger potatoes may need up to 75 minutes.
3. Split the potatoes immediately. Scoop out the flesh, pass it through a ricer onto a wide work surface, and weigh 750 g. Reserve any excess for another use. Spread the riced potato loosely and let steam escape for 3–5 minutes; work while it is still warm.
4. Drizzle the yolk over the potato and distribute the salt evenly. Scatter 130 g of the flour over everything. Cut and fold gently with a bench scraper just until a dough begins to form.
5. Add the remaining 20 g flour only while bringing the dough together. Cut and fold just two or three times, stopping as soon as the mass holds together; do not knead it like bread or pasta dough. The dough should be cohesive, slightly tacky, and soft rather than smooth or elastic.
6. Dust the bench lightly with rice flour or semolina. Divide the dough, roll into ropes 18–20 mm thick, and cut into 20 mm pieces. Leave plain or roll once over a gnocchi board without compressing them.
7. Test-cook one gnocco in gently simmering salted water. If it feathers apart, fold 5 g additional flour into the dough; avoid adding flour merely because the surface feels tacky.
8. Cook in batches at a bare simmer. Once the gnocchi float, continue for 30–45 seconds, then lift directly into the finished sauce. Toss gently for 30–60 seconds and serve immediately.

## Notes

- Use mature, floury potatoes rather than waxy new potatoes. Baking them and ricing while hot minimizes free water and therefore the flour required.
- The formula is based on the weight of cooked potato flesh: flour is 20%, yolk 4.8%, and salt 1.3%.
- Handle the dough for the minimum time possible. It does not need to become smooth: gluten development and compressed potato both make gnocchi dense or elastic.
- Freeze shaped gnocchi in one layer, then bag once solid. Cook from frozen without thawing.
